# DIBO-JP-0004 — Optional separate surnames for married couples

## Scope

This registered lineage begins with the Supreme Court Grand Bench judgment of 2015-12-16. That judgment maintained the constitutionality of Civil Code Article 750 while acknowledging disadvantages associated with surname change and expressly distinguishing constitutional review from the legislative-policy question of what surname system Japan should adopt. This makes it a defensible modern institutional boundary for following later judicial reconsideration and legislative or governmental responses.

The earlier history of Article 750, prior Diet proposals, advocacy, international recommendations, and local-assembly opinions is background rather than canonical Episodes in this deliberately small v1.0 lineage.

## Why this is one Issue Lineage

The two Grand Bench matters, the three 2025 member bills, their common committee treatment, and the 2026 government plan all address the same underlying problem: whether and how the legal and administrative consequences of surname change on marriage should be altered. They do not offer equivalent answers. Bills 29 and 35 proposed optional separate legal surnames with different child-surname and family-register designs. Bill 30 retained the same-surname rule and proposed statutory support for use of a pre-marriage surname. The Sixth Basic Plan likewise opened a government pathway for considering expanded pre-marriage-surname use while leaving the legal rule unchanged.

## Timeline

- 2015-12-16 — `J` — The Supreme Court Grand Bench upheld Civil Code Article 750, acknowledged surname-change disadvantages, and distinguished constitutional review from legislative policy.
- 2021-06-23 — `J` — The Supreme Court Grand Bench maintained the 2015 conclusion despite social and public-attitude changes and again identified system design as a matter for Diet debate and decision.
- 2025-04-30 — `L` — House Member Bill No. 29 proposed optional separate legal surnames and designation at marriage of the surname children would use.
- 2025-05-19 — `L` — House Member Bill No. 30 retained the same-surname rule and proposed a statutory framework for registered use of a pre-marriage surname as a common-use name.
- 2025-05-28 — `L` — House Member Bill No. 35 proposed optional separate legal surnames, designation of a family-register head, and use of that person's surname by the children.
- 2025-06-20 — `L` — The House Committee on Judicial Affairs decided to request continued examination during the recess for Bills 29, 30, and 35 without adopting any of them.
- 2026-03-13 — `A` — The Cabinet adopted the Sixth Basic Plan for Gender Equality, including consideration of infrastructure for expanded pre-marriage-surname use, potentially including single-name use, and continued study of the spouses' surname system.

## Status

Civil Code Article 750 continues to require spouses to select one shared legal surname on marriage. The official House list for the 220th Diet marks Bills 29, 30, and 35 as unfinished. The 221st Diet bill list contains no reintroduced equivalent of those proposals and no government bill implementing expanded pre-marriage-surname use. No optional separate legal-surname system has been enacted.

The Sixth Basic Plan for Gender Equality directs continued consideration of legal and other infrastructure for wider pre-marriage-surname use, including possible single-name use, and continued study of the spouses' surname system. That policy does not itself amend Article 750 and is not equivalent to allowing spouses to retain separate legal surnames.

## Methodological note

1. The Supreme Court's statements that legislative policy should be debated and decided by the Diet are not coded as a formal institutional referral.
2. Legislative uptake is reconstructed from the later bill-submission Episodes, without claiming that the judgments caused those bills.
3. The three bills remain separate Episodes because their legal mechanisms differ.
4. Their common 2025-06-20 committee treatment is one Episode because it was one observable institutional action; it does not merge the bills.
5. Expanded legal or common-use treatment of a pre-marriage surname under the same-surname system is not treated as equivalent to optional separate legal surnames.
6. The lineage's long unresolved duration is not, by itself, coded or described as Institutional Runaround.
